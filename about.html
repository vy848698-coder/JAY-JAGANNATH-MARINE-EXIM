Process & Compliance

What We Mean by Classified

Raw ash is inconsistent. Classification separates the fine fraction, so what leaves our yard is a graded product with a test certificate against it — not whatever the boiler produced that week.

Why It Matters

Consistency Is the Product

The properties of fly ash vary widely, both physically and chemically, with the coal burned and the efficiency of combustion. Two loads from the same station in the same month can behave differently in a mix.

By classifying and processing, we ensure an optimum proportion of our fly ash is composed of fine particles — which is what makes the material controlled and consistent rather than merely available.

Fineness is the single property that governs how fly ash performs. Finer particles react faster, pack better and give the strength development that concrete specifications are written around.

The Numbers That Decide

Maximum Residue on the 45 μm Sieve

Fineness is measured as the percentage of material left behind on a 45 micron sieve after wet sieving. The lower the residue, the finer the ash.

Bench to Bag

How a Consignment Is Classified

Step 01

Collection at Source

Dry ash is drawn from the electrostatic precipitator hoppers of the thermal station, kept separate from bottom ash and pond ash from the outset.

Step 02

Air Classification

The ash passes through a classifier that separates it by particle size, splitting off the coarse fraction and leaving the fines that carry the pozzolanic reactivity.

Step 03

Fineness Testing

Samples are wet sieved on the 45 μm sieve to establish residue, with specific surface confirmed by the Blaine air permeability method — minimum 320 m²/kg under IS 3812.

Step 04

Certification and Release

A test certificate is issued against ASTM C618, BS EN 450 S or IS 3812 for the lot, and only then is the consignment packed and released for despatch.
